Energy in, energy out

A battery sends energy into a circuit. Where does the energy go? Here you can see the energy as little dots. Energy is counted in joules (J). You will count the joules that go in. You will count the joules that come out.

The load is the part that uses the energy. A bulb is a load.

When you finish, you can:

  • Tell what kinds of energy come out of a load.
  • Show that joules out = joules in.
  • Use a thermometer to find heat.
  • Read a Sankey diagram.

The numbers here are pretend. Everyone sees the same numbers.

2. Take the temperature

Heat is hard to see. A thermometer can find it. Drag the thermometer by its red tip. Read the number. A room is about 22 °C.

Buttons:

  2. 2Click the switch to close it. Wait until the number is over 50 °C.
  3. 3Drag the red tip onto the top wire. Wait until the number stops changing.
  4. 4Drag the red tip onto the battery. Wait until the number stops changing.
  5. 5Drag the red tip back onto the bulb.
  6. 6Click the switch to open it. Wait until the bulb is under 40 °C.

Notice

The wire and the battery get a tiny bit warm. That heat is very small. We do not count it.
