Measure resistance with a multimeter

Resistance tells you how hard it is for electric current to flow through something. A multimeter measures it. You will learn how, then test parts and wires to see what changes resistance.

When you finish, you can:

  • Set up a multimeter to measure resistance.
  • Read the number and the units.
  • Explain what changes resistance: material, width, and length.

Have a real multimeter? Follow along with it. Use the virtual one on these slides to practice. Readings are simulated so every class sees the same numbers.

    2. Set up the meter

    Safety rule

    Never measure resistance on a part that has power. Take it out of the circuit first. Power can damage the meter.

    Good to know

    On a real meter, probes touching may read a little more than 0, like 0.2 Ω. That is the resistance of the meter's own wires.

    Facts: materials

    Different materials have different amounts of resistance. Some materials don't allow current to flow through easily, these are called insulators. Other materials, called conductors, allow current to flow through easily. Metals are conductors. Some metals have more resistance than others. Copper, aluminum, and gold have very low resistance and are excellent conductors of electricity. Copper is most commonly used in large circuits like those found in buildings and devices because it is stronger than aluminum and less expensive than gold.

    Look at your numbers: copper, aluminum, and gold read low. These are great conductors. Nichrome reads high. It is a metal that resists current a lot.

    Some metals have more resistance than others. Copper, aluminum, and gold have very low resistance and are excellent conductors of electricity. Copper is most commonly used in large circuits like those found in buildings and devices because it is stronger than aluminum and less expensive than gold.

    Mission B: Try different widths

    Pick copper and 25 m. Switch between thin and wide. Compare the numbers.

    Wire shape also can impact resistance. Wide wires have lots of space for current. This means that wide wires have lower resistance. Skinnier wires don't have as much room for the current. They have higher resistance. This is why power lines that you see outside are much wider than the wires you see in your home. The power lines need to carry more current so more space is needed to prevent overheating.

    Mission C: Try different lengths

    Pick copper and thin. Switch between 5 m, 25 m, and 100 m. Compare the numbers.

    Length also impacts resistance. Longer wires resist the flow of current more than shorter wires. This is another reason power lines need wide wires. Power lines are very long when compared to the wires in your home.
