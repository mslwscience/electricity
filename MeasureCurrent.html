Measure current with a multimeter

Current is how much charge flows past a point each second. To measure it, the meter has to be part of the loop, so the charges flow right through it. You will set up a multimeter for current, drop an ammeter into a circuit, and see how PhET's ammeter works.

When you finish, you can:

  • Set up a multimeter to measure current.
  • Put a meter in the loop to measure current.
  • Explain why an ammeter goes inside the loop but a voltmeter goes directly on either side of a part.

Have a real multimeter? Follow along with it, but ask an adult before you measure current. Readings here are simulated so every class sees the same numbers.

1. Current goes through the meter

A voltmeter goes directly on either side of a part. Current is different. To count the charges flowing, the meter has to be in the loop, so every charge passes through it.

Where does the meter go?

Voltage: on either side of the part
One probe goes directly on each side of the bulb. This is called parallel.
Current: inside the loop
Open the loop and let the meter fill the gap. This is called series.

Dial on A

A stands for amps, the unit for current.

Red lead in 10A

For current, the red lead moves to the 10A port. The black lead stays in COM.

Safety: never across a battery

On A, the meter acts like a plain wire. Put it across a battery and you make a short circuit. It can blow the meter's fuse or get hot.

2. Set up the meter and measure current

Before you start

Someone just used this meter for voltage. The circuit is open at the gap between P and Q. The meter will close it.

  1. 1Turn the dial to A. (Tap the A on the meter.)
  2. 2Drag the red plug out of VΩ and into the 10A port. The black plug stays in COM.
  3. 3Drag the red probe tip to P, the end of the gap that leads back to the + end of the battery.
  4. 4Drag the black probe tip to Q, the other end of the gap. Watch the bulb.
  5. 5Read the number and units: A

4. Drop an ammeter into a circuit

An ammeter is a meter that only measures current. Its symbol is a circle with an A. Drag it onto a wire to drop it into the loop.

  1. 1Drop the ammeter onto the top wire. Read the current.
  2. 2Move it to the bottom wire. Did the reading change?
  3. 3Switch to 2 bulbs. Drop the ammeter between the bulbs.
  4. 4Try the wrong way: drop it across a bulb (on the dashed spot next to the bulb). What happens?

Remember

An ammeter acts like a plain wire. Put it in the loop, never on either side of a part.

5. Measuring without opening the loop

Electricians also use a clamp meter. Its jaws close around a wire and sense the current inside it, so they never have to open the loop. PhET's Ammeter works like a clamp meter: you put its circle on a wire.

In the PhET Circuit Construction Kit

  • Drag the Ammeter from the box at the top right.
  • Put its circle on any wire. It reads the current in that wire.
  • You do not need to open the loop or move any plugs.

Three ways, same answer

A multimeter in the loop, an ammeter dropped in the loop, or a clamp around the wire: all three measure the same current.
